Does burning early stop a late fire the next year?

Early dry-season burning programmes run on the premise that a cool fire before August removes the fuel that would carry a damaging one later. Each bar is the chance of a late-season fire next year.

What the forest leans on

Weather in ember, fire history in slate.

Nothing sees the year it predicts

Fire-history features share a table with the label, so an off-by-one would hand a model the answer. Two tests guard it: sampled features are recomputed from the raw record, and a year's labels are scrambled to confirm none of its features move.

Weather stops at 30 April

The fire season runs May to October. August weather would describe a fire instead of anticipating it. Everything the model reads is known once the wet season ends, so it can be run in May.

The month is approximate

NAFI gives each burnt pixel the month covering most of the window in which it was detected. Near the July boundary that attribution blurs, which limits how sharp the early and late split can be.
